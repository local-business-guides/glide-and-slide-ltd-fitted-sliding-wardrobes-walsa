Planning an alcove wardrobe that works for you

Before you choose an alcove wardrobe, think about what will go inside it and how you'll move around it. Those decisions matter just as much as the door finish.

Check what your alcove wardrobe needs to hold

Start with the clothes you use each week. Long coats need a different arrangement from shirts, and folded knitwear won't benefit from another hanging rail. Take a quick inventory before discussing shelves or drawers. You don't need to count every sock, but knowing which items take up the most room gives the design a useful starting point.

Think about access, too. Storage for everyday clothing should be comfortable to reach. Bedding and occasional items can sit elsewhere in the layout, provided you can retrieve them safely. If two people will share the wardrobe, explain how you'd like the space divided rather than assuming equal sections will suit both of you.

Understand the recess before choosing an alcove wardrobe

An opening can look square without having straight sides. Skirting boards, a chimney breast and changes in wall depth can all affect how the furniture fits. Preliminary measurements help a conversation, but they shouldn't become the final manufacturing dimensions without a proper assessment.

Look beyond the opening itself. A radiator beside the recess, an electrical socket behind it or a nearby bedroom door may influence the design. Tell the designer about these details early. If something needs moving, establish who will arrange that work before installation is booked.

Door choice deserves the same attention. Sliding doors don't swing into the room, but the internal arrangement still needs to work with the openings. Ask how you'll reach drawers and hanging sections when the doors are in different positions.

Prepare for your alcove wardrobe consultation

Bring information that helps explain your routine, rather than only collecting pictures of attractive bedrooms. A finish you like is useful, but a clear account of your storage problems is more useful still.

Have these details ready:

  • Photos showing the full recess and the furniture around it.
  • Approximate width, height and depth, with any obvious variations noted.
  • Examples of bulky items, long garments or equipment you need to store.
  • The position of sockets, switches, radiators and nearby doors.
  • A few finish preferences and an idea of your available budget.

Ask what the proposed work includes. Confirm the doors, internal fittings, installation and any preparation expected from you. Written details make it easier to compare proposals on the same basis and spot an omission before it becomes a problem.

Avoid common alcove wardrobe planning mistakes

Don't fill every section with shelves simply because they look tidy in a drawing. Shelves that are too deep can hide clothing at the back, while closely spaced compartments may leave no room for larger belongings. Explain what each section needs to do and ask to see how the layout supports that use.

Questions people ask about alcove wardrobe

Can an alcove wardrobe include hanging space and drawers?

A fitted layout can combine different storage sections, depending on the available dimensions. Tell the designer what you want to store so drawer access and hanging space can be considered together.

Should I decorate before having an alcove wardrobe installed?

Ask about the installation sequence before arranging decorating. Confirm which surfaces will remain visible and what preparation is needed so you can plan the work in a sensible order.

What should I ask when comparing alcove wardrobe proposals?

Check the dimensions, internal layout, door specification and installation scope. Ask each supplier to identify anything you must arrange separately, rather than comparing appearance alone.
